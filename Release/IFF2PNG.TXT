===========================================================================
   1. INTRODUCTION
===========================================================================

   1.1. What IFF2PNG is

   IFF2PNG is a command line converter that transforms graphics files
   in IFF format -- both the chunky PBM variant and the interleaved
   planar ILBM variant -- into PNG format. It is the successor of
   PBM2PNG and, like it, was developed as a support tool for X-VESA:
   the VESA diagnostic program saves screenshots in IFF-PBM format, a
   native format from the 1980s not directly readable by most modern
   PC applications. IFF2PNG fills this gap by producing standard PNG
   files, compatible with any current viewer, browser or graphics
   application.

   Unlike PBM2PNG, IFF2PNG is not limited to X-VESA screenshots. It
   also reads the files produced by the Amiga paint and capture
   programs of the period: indexed images of 1 to 8 bitplanes, Extra
   Half-Brite (EHB), HAM6 and HAM8, pictures whose palette changes on
   every scanline (Sliced HAM, Dynamic Hires, Dynamic HAM), 24-plane
   deep images and images with a mask plane, which are converted to
   RGBA PNG.

   IFF2PNG is written in C89/C90 (with a 64-bit integer extension) and
   has no dependencies on external libraries: the DEFLATE encoder, the
   CRC-32 and Adler-32 checksums and the PNG writer are all part of the
   program. The executable prints a banner such as

     IFF2PNG v20.6.5 [Windows x64 Multi-Thread]

   showing the version, the operating system, the CPU architecture
   and whether the build can compress on multiple threads.

   IFF2PNG compresses its filter candidates in parallel across multiple
   CPU cores where a threading API is available (Win32 or POSIX).
   Every candidate is compressed independently, so the number of
   threads changes how fast the conversion completes, never what it
   produces.


   1.2. Features

   - Reading of FORM PBM and FORM ILBM files with BMHD, CMAP, CAMG and
     BODY chunks, in big-endian format conforming to the IFF
     specification, with validation of chunk order, chunk sizes and
     ByteRun1 packet boundaries.

   - Support for PackBits/ByteRun1 compression (type 1) and
     uncompressed files (type 0), for both PBM and ILBM.

   - FORM PBM: palettized images at 8 bits per pixel (CMAP required)
     and true color images at 24 bits per pixel.

   - FORM ILBM: 1 to 8 bitplanes (indexed), Extra Half-Brite (EHB),
     HAM6, HAM8 and 24-plane deep RGB images (plane 0..7 = red,
     8..15 = green, 16..23 = blue, least significant bit first).

   - Per-line palettes: SHAM (Sliced HAM) and CTBL (NewTek Dynamic
     Hires and Dynamic HAM) chunks, which store a 16-color palette for
     every scanline. The palette of each row is applied before the row
     is decoded, for indexed, EHB and HAM pictures; the result is a
     truecolor PNG, or an indexed one when the picture has at most 256
     colors (section 3.4).

   - Masking support: color-key transparency (masking 2) for PBM and
     ILBM, emitted as a PNG tRNS chunk; mask plane (masking 1) for
     ILBM, converted to a per-pixel alpha channel and written as RGBA
     PNG.

   - Automatic conversion from true color to indexed palette: if the
     24-bit image (PBM 24-bit, 24-plane ILBM, or the RGB result of a
     HAM or per-line palette decode) contains at most 256 distinct
     colors, IFF2PNG converts it to an indexed PNG, significantly
     reducing the output file size. The transparent color, if present,
     is carried over to the new palette.

   - Automatic PNG color depth selection based on the number of
     colors actually used: 1 bit (up to 2 colors), 2 bits (up to 4
     colors), 4 bits (up to 16 colors), 8 bits (up to 256 colors).
     Pixel packing at 1, 2 and 4 bits is performed internally.

   - Palette compaction: the PLTE chunk contains only the colors that
     the image really uses, so a 256-entry CMAP with five colors in
     use produces a 5-entry PLTE.

   - Palette de-duplication: if the palette entries in use contain
     duplicate RGB values, IFF2PNG builds two candidates (with and
     without merging the duplicates), compares them with a fast probe
     and keeps the smaller one. Can be disabled (-ndedup) or forced
     (-fdedup).

   - Palette ordering optimization for images with at most 16 colors:
     IFF2PNG reorders the palette to minimize index variations between
     adjacent pixels, improving the efficiency of PNG filters and
     subsequent DEFLATE compression (adjacency matrix on horizontally
     contiguous pixels, greedy path construction from every start
     color and 2-opt refinement).

   - Whole-image filter competition: rather than committing to a
     single filtering strategy, IFF2PNG fully DEFLATE-compresses
     several candidate filterings of the same image -- per-row
     adaptive selection, and fixed None and Up filters applied
     uniformly (plus fixed Sub and Paeth when the PNG depth is above
     2 bits) -- and keeps the candidate that produced the smallest
     real compressed output. Candidates that reduce to byte-identical
     filtered data are detected and compressed only once.

   - Two selectable DEFLATE parsers:
       . a fast two-pass lazy-matching LZ77 parser with configurable
         hash-chain search depth (-c1 to -c4);
       . a dynamic-programming shortest-path parser (-cx) that
         searches the token graph using estimated Huffman bit costs,
         instead of a greedy/lazy decision at each position.

   - Candidate pruning with -cx: on large images a cheap fast-engine
     pre-pass discards the filter candidates that are clearly worse,
     so that the expensive DP parser runs only where it can matter.
     Can be disabled with -nprune.

   - Huffman tables built with the Package-Merge algorithm for
     length-limited canonical prefix codes (max 15 bits for the
     literal/length and distance alphabets, max 7 bits for the code-
     length alphabet), with an explicit Kraft-McMillan verification
     on every table built.

   - Adaptive multi-block DEFLATE partitioning: the token stream can
     be split into several independently Huffman-coded blocks (up to
     8). Each block is written with a dynamic Huffman table or, when
     it is shorter, with the fixed Huffman code of RFC 1951. Splitting
     can be disabled with -nbs.

   - Automatic stored-block (BTYPE=00) fallback: if the whole dynamic
     stream would be larger than an uncompressed zlib stream, IFF2PNG
     writes the uncompressed stream instead, as allowed by RFC 1951.

   - Tolerant parsing of three common writer quirks, each reported as a
     warning, and a -strict mode that turns all of them into errors
     (see section 2.6).

   - Multithreaded compression: on Win32/Win64 and on POSIX systems
     each distinct filter candidate is compressed on its own thread;
     on platforms without a supported threading API the same
     algorithms run sequentially with identical results.

   - Optional diagnostic log (-v) with the details of the input file,
     the decode path, the palette, the PNG produced, the filters, the
     compression and the timing.

   - PNG file writing conforming to the specification, with IHDR,
     PLTE (for indexed images), tRNS (when transparency is present),
     a single IDAT and IEND chunk, CRC-32 for each chunk and Adler-32
     in the zlib stream.


   1.3. The IFF formats and the PNG format

   The IFF (Interchange File Format) was developed by Electronic Arts
   and Commodore in the 1980s for the Amiga platform. A bitmap is
   stored as a FORM container holding a sequence of chunks identified
   by four bytes: BMHD (header with resolution, color depth, masking
   and compression type), CMAP (RGB palette), CAMG (Amiga display mode
   flags, ILBM only) and BODY (image data, optionally compressed with
   PackBits/ByteRun1). All multi-byte values are in big-endian format.

   Two image layouts are accepted:

   - PBM (Packed BitMap): chunky layout, one byte per pixel (8 bit
     indexed) or three bytes per pixel (24 bit RGB). This is the
     format written by X-VESA.

   - ILBM (InterLeaved BitMap): planar layout. Each scanline is stored
     as a sequence of bitplanes, each plane padded to a 16-bit word
     boundary, optionally followed by a mask plane. This is the
     classic Amiga format and the one used by HAM and EHB images.

   X-VESA uses the IFF-PBM format for its screenshots because its
   structure is straightforward to generate entirely in assembly
   language, without dependencies on external libraries, and is
   compatible with the Amiga viewers of the era for which the program
   is designed.

   The PNG (Portable Network Graphics) format is the de facto open
   standard for lossless raster graphics on PC and on the web. It is
   natively supported by all modern browsers, operating systems and
   graphics applications, and is therefore the natural format for
   making IFF images accessible to a wide audience.


===========================================================================
   2. HOW TO USE IFF2PNG
===========================================================================

   2.1. Command line syntax

   IFF2PNG <IN.IFF> <OUT.PNG> [-c1..-c4] [-cx] [-nbs] [-ndedup]
           [-fdedup] [-nprune] [-strict] [-v]

   IN.IFF       Source file in IFF format (FORM PBM or FORM ILBM).
                The file name extension is not checked.
   OUT.PNG      Destination file in PNG format. An existing file is
                overwritten.
   [option]     Any combination of the options below.

   Parameters can be specified in any order. The input file and the
   output file are identified as the first two arguments that do not
   begin with the '-' character. If either file is missing, IFF2PNG
   displays the syntax and exits. A third argument that does not begin
   with '-' is rejected, and so is any unknown option.

   Options are matched exactly and are case sensitive. If more than one
   of -c1..-c4 is given, the last one wins. The options -ndedup and
   -fdedup are mutually exclusive.

   In case of error reading the input file (file not found,
   unrecognized format, missing BMHD or BODY chunks, invalid chunk
   ordering or size, invalid resolution, unsupported depth or masking,
   illegal palette index, malformed ByteRun1 data) IFF2PNG reports the
   specific problem on stderr and exits without producing output.

   Summary of the options:

     -c1 .. -c4   Chain search depth of the DEFLATE parser (2.2).
     -cx          Dynamic-programming parser (2.2).
     -nbs         Disable DEFLATE block splitting (2.3).
     -ndedup      Disable palette de-duplication (2.4).
     -fdedup      Force palette de-duplication (2.4).
     -nprune      With -cx, do not prune filter candidates (2.5).
     -strict      Follow the IFF standard to the letter (2.6).
     -v           Write a diagnostic log file (2.7).


   2.2. Compression levels and engine selection

   -c1    Search depth 4,096 positions.
          Fastest compression, larger file size.

   -c2    Search depth 8,192 positions.

   -c3    Search depth 16,384 positions.

   -c4    Search depth 32,768 positions (default).
          Slowest of the two-pass engine's levels, minimum file size
          among -c1..-c4.

   -cx    Ultra mode: replaces the two-pass lazy matcher with a
          dynamic-programming (DP) parser that computes, for every
          position of the data, the cheapest way of reaching it with a
          literal or a match, using estimated Huffman bit costs, and
          then follows the cheapest path back from the end. Produces
          the smallest output IFF2PNG is capable of, at a
          significantly higher CPU cost. -cx can be combined with
          -c1..-c4: the chain-search depth then bounds the DP
          matcher's candidate search rather than the lazy matcher's
          (without an explicit level the depth is 32,768).

   The default level -c4 offers the best compression among the fast
   engine's levels and is suitable for typical use. Lower levels are
   useful when converting large numbers of files on slow systems.
   -cx is recommended when file size matters more than conversion
   time, for example for a screenshot meant for permanent archival
   or publication.

   -cx is a cost-model search, not a proof of optimality: the cost
   it minimizes is the sum of the estimated code lengths and extra
   bits of the tokens, without the block headers and without the
   effect of block splitting, and the match search relaxes only the
   lengths that are longer than the best length already seen at a
   position (see section 3.8). The gap between -c4 and -cx is usually
   small for the typical images generated by X-VESA, but it is not
   guaranteed to be, and the result of -cx is not guaranteed to be
   smaller than the result of -c4 on every image.


   2.3. Block splitting

   Block splitting is enabled by default. IFF2PNG looks for genuine
   statistical changes in the compressed token stream -- for example
   the boundary between a text panel and a flat-colored border -- and,
   where beneficial, encodes the regions on either side as separate
   Huffman blocks within the same IDAT stream. See section 3.9.

   -nbs   Disables block splitting: the whole image is encoded as a
          single block (dynamic Huffman, or fixed Huffman if that is
          shorter). The option also applies to the size probe used by
          palette de-duplication.

   Each split is accepted only if the modelled cost of the two blocks
   is lower than the cost of the single block by more than 16 bits.
   Whether block splitting improves on -nbs for a specific image
   depends on its content and is best judged by comparing the two on
   the actual file. The search is a heuristic and does not find a
   global optimum.

   Note: PBM2PNG had an explicit -bs option. IFF2PNG does not: block
   splitting is the default and only -nbs exists. A command line that
   contains -bs is rejected with "Unknown option".


   2.4. Palette de-duplication

   Some programs write palettes in which several entries have the same
   RGB value, so that two visually identical areas of the image use
   different indices. Merging such entries can reduce the number of
   colors, lower the PNG bit depth and make the index stream more
   regular. It can also make the file larger, because it changes the
   data that DEFLATE has to compress. IFF2PNG therefore decides by
   measurement.

   (default)  If two palette entries that the image really uses have
              the same RGB value, two candidates are built -- with and
              without merging -- and each one is compressed with the
              fast engine (adaptive filtering, chain limit 4,096,
              whatever -c1..-c4 and -cx are set to). The candidate
              with the smaller IDAT + PLTE + tRNS total is kept.
              If there are no duplicates among the entries in use, or
              the merge would not reduce the number of colors, the
              comparison is skipped.

   -ndedup    The comparison is not performed and duplicate entries are
              never merged.

   -fdedup    The merge is applied without any size comparison.

   The transparent index of an image with color-key transparency
   (masking 2) is never merged with another entry. Images that were
   reduced from true color to indexed by IFF2PNG have a palette that is
   unique by construction, so no de-duplication is needed for them.


   2.5. Candidate pruning with -cx

   The DP parser is several times slower than the fast engine, and it
   is run once for every distinct filter candidate. To limit the cost,
   when -cx is selected and the filtered scanlines of the image are at
   least 128 KiB, IFF2PNG first compresses every distinct candidate
   with the fast engine (chain limit 64, no block splitting; about 5%
   of the cost of a DP run). The candidates whose fast size is more
   than 8% larger than the best fast size do not go on to the DP
   parser: they keep their fast stream as their result and still take
   part in the final selection. At least three distinct candidates
   are required for pruning to be active.

   -nprune  Disables the pre-pass: the DP parser is run on every
            distinct candidate (exhaustive, slower).

   Pruning has no effect without -cx, on small images, or when fewer
   than three distinct candidates exist.


   2.6. Tolerant and strict parsing

   By default the FORM parser is tolerant: three common writer quirks
   are accepted. Each one is reported with a warning on stderr.

   - X-VESA last-chunk pad: the FORM size excludes the pad byte of the
     last odd-sized chunk, but exactly one byte follows the FORM at
     the end of the file. Accepted only if the chunk ends exactly at
     the end of the FORM and the file is exactly one byte longer. Any
     pad value is accepted; the value is always reported, and a
     non-zero value is flagged.

   - BODY size includes the pad byte: when the ByteRun1 stream has an
     odd length, some writers include the pad byte in the BODY size.
     Accepted when exactly one byte remains after a complete decode
     (ILBM and PBM). Any value is accepted; a non-zero value is
     flagged.

   - Masking 1 without mask plane: an uncompressed ILBM that declares
     masking 1 but whose BODY size matches the colour planes only is
     accepted as masking 0.

   -strict  Follows the IFF standard to the letter: all three
            conditions are hard errors. When one of the first two
            quirks is rejected, the error message notes that it is
            tolerated by default.

   The three tolerances are deferred warnings: they are printed after
   the progress line, so that they never interleave with it. On an
   error, they are printed before the error message. Other warnings
   (for instance the heuristics of section 3.3) are printed as soon as
   they are detected. The assumptions made on files without a CAMG
   chunk (HAM6, EHB and SHAM) and the anomalies of SHAM and CTBL chunks
   (section 3.4) do not depend on -strict.


   2.7. The diagnostic log (-v)

   -v  Writes a diagnostic log file. The name of the log is the input
       path with its extension replaced by ".log" (INPUT.IFF produces
       INPUT.log; a name with no extension gets ".log" appended). The
       log is written after a successful conversion; a failure to
       create it is reported as a warning and does not affect the
       conversion.

   The log has format version 1.0. The first lines are the banner and
   the title; then follow the sections:

     [0] INVOCATION   Command line as received, compression engine,
                      chain limit, block splitting, de-duplication
                      mode, pruning, parser mode (tolerant/strict),
                      probe parameters, OS, architecture, threading.
     [1] INPUT FILE   Input and output names, file and FORM sizes,
                      trailing bytes, IFF type (PBM/ILBM), chunk
                      inventory with sizes, non-standard chunks
                      (with the name of the format or program when
                      known: DPXT, CTBL, SHAM, PCHG, DYCP, CRNG).
     [2] IMAGE HEADER Dimensions, source depth, IFF compression, BMHD
                      flags, masking declared and effective,
                      transparent color index, CAMG presence, value and
                      decoded flags (a junk high word is reported),
                      display flags (HIRES, LACE), color
                      interpretation (indexed, EHB, HAM6, HAM8, deep
                      RGB, RGBA and per-line palette variants).
     [3] PALETTE      CMAP entries as stored, palette bit depth, 4-bit
                      heuristic status, EHB expansion, indices in use,
                      unique RGB colors, de-duplication mode, result
                      and outcome, final palette entries, palette
                      reordering.
     [4] DECODE PATH  Decode branch, auto-detections applied (HAM6 or
                      EHB without CAMG, 4-bit CMAP), fallbacks applied
                      (masking 1 -> 0, color-key dropped), 24-to-8
                      quantization, index validation, color-key
                      resolution, per-line palettes (SHAM or CTBL
                      used, palettes stored, image rows covered,
                      interlace skip, every warning issued about the
                      chunks; or why they were not applied).
     [5] PNG OUTPUT   Internal format, PNG color type and depth,
                      palette entries, pixels, size of the PNG file and
                      of each chunk, ratios against the raw pixels and
                      against the IFF FORM.
     [6] FILTERING    Winning strategy, scanlines per filter type,
                      most frequent filter, number of distinct
                      candidates compressed, number of candidates
                      pruned.
     [7] COMPRESSION  Engine, chain limit, bytes given to DEFLATE,
                      tokens, literals, matches, match coverage,
                      average and maximum match, block splitting
                      figures (candidate points evaluated, splits
                      accepted, final blocks, single-block size, split
                      total, gain), Huffman symbol counts and maximum
                      code lengths. The figures refer to the candidate
                      that was selected.
     [8] TIMING       Time spent in IFF parse+decode, palette analysis,
                      filtering, LZ77 + DEFLATE and PNG assembly,
                      with percentages, and the total.
     [9] TOLERANCES   Parser mode and the state of each tolerance
                      (X-VESA pad, BODY pad, masking fallback, with
                      file offsets and pad values), CAMG junk.
     [SUMMARY]        A short recapitulation of the conversion.

   The log is intended for diagnosing unusual files and for comparing
   the effect of the options on a given image. V20.7.0 added the
   display flags line to [2] and the per-line palette lines to [4],
   V20.7.1 the per-line palette notes line; the rest of the format is
   unchanged.


   2.8. Console output and exit status

   IFF2PNG first prints the banner. During compression, if the
   standard output is a terminal, a progress bar is drawn and updated
   on a single line:

     Compressing: [=============>          ]  54%

   When the PNG file has been written the bar is completed and the line
   ends with the elapsed time, for example "100% - Time elapsed: 1.23
   seconds". If the standard output is redirected to a file
   or a pipe the bar is not drawn and a single line

     Compressing: done - Time elapsed: 1.23 seconds

   is printed instead. Warnings and errors are written on stderr.

   The exit status is 0 when the PNG file was written and 1 in every
   other case (wrong arguments, unreadable or invalid input, out of
   memory, workspace limit exceeded, thread creation failure, write
   failure or disk full while writing the output).


   2.9. Usage examples

   Simple conversion with default settings (-c4, block splitting on):

     IFF2PNG FILE0001.IFF FILE0001.PNG

   Conversion with minimum compression level (maximum speed):

     IFF2PNG FILE0001.IFF FILE0001.PNG -c1

   Conversion with the dynamic-programming parser, for the smallest
   possible file:

     IFF2PNG FILE0001.IFF FILE0001.PNG -cx

   Conversion with block splitting disabled:

     IFF2PNG FILE0001.IFF FILE0001.PNG -c4 -nbs

   -cx without candidate pruning, with a diagnostic log:

     IFF2PNG FILE0001.IFF FILE0001.PNG -cx -nprune -v

   Conversion of a Sliced HAM or Dynamic Hires picture (the SHAM and
   CTBL chunks are recognized automatically; the log tells how the
   per-line palettes were applied):

     IFF2PNG DYNHIRES.IFF DYNHIRES.PNG -cx -v

   Strict parsing of a file that must follow the IFF standard:

     IFF2PNG FILE0001.IFF FILE0001.PNG -strict

   Under Windows it is possible to convert all IFF files in the
   current directory via a batch file:

     FOR %%F IN (*.IFF) DO IFF2PNG %%F %%~nF.PNG -cx

   Under a Unix shell:

     for f in *.iff; do iff2png "$f" "${f%.iff}.png" -cx; done


===========================================================================
   3. TECHNICAL DETAILS
===========================================================================

   3.1. Reading the IFF file

   IFF2PNG reads the entire file into memory (files larger than 4 GiB
   are refused) and parses its chunk structure. The presence of the
   FORM header with type PBM or ILBM is verified; any other type is
   rejected. The size declared in the FORM header must be at least 4
   and must fit in the file.

   Chunks are read in sequence from offset 12 up to the end of the
   FORM. Only BMHD, CMAP, CAMG, BODY, SHAM and CTBL are interpreted;
   every other chunk is skipped. BMHD, CMAP, CAMG and BODY are each
   rejected if duplicated or found out of the order required by the IFF
   specification (CMAP, CAMG and BODY may not precede BMHD; BMHD, CMAP
   and CAMG may not follow BODY). A chunk whose declared size exceeds
   the space left in the FORM is reported as truncated. Word padding
   (the alignment byte for odd-size chunks) is handled correctly; a
   missing pad byte is an error, except for the X-VESA quirk of section
   2.6. Bytes left inside the FORM that do not make up a chunk are
   treated as a corrupt file rather than silently ignored. Bytes after
   the end of the FORM (for example a container wrapper or appended
   metadata) are ignored.

   SHAM and CTBL (section 3.4) are not part of the IFF standard, so an
   unexpected one is never an error: a duplicate is ignored (the first
   one is kept) and one that follows BODY is ignored, each with a
   warning. Their position relative to BMHD and CMAP does not matter.

   From the BMHD chunk (at least 20 bytes) the following are
   extracted: width and height (16 bit each), number of bitplanes or
   color depth, masking, compression type, the Flags byte and the
   transparent color index. Bit 7 of the Flags byte is BMHDF_CMAPOK:
   the writer declares an intentional 8 bit per channel CMAP (see
   section 3.3). A width or height of zero, or the absence of BODY,
   is reported as an incomplete file.

   The accepted values are:

     FORM PBM    depth 8 or 24; masking 0 or 2.
                 Masking 1 (stencil plane) is not supported in PBM.
     FORM ILBM   depth 1..8 or 24; masking 0, 1 or 2.
     Both        compression 0 (none) or 1 (ByteRun1).

   The CMAP chunk must be 3 to 768 bytes long and a multiple of 3
   (1 to 256 colors). The CAMG chunk (ILBM only) must be at least 4
   bytes long; if its high word is non-zero while EXTENDED_MODE
   (bit 12) is clear, a warning is printed ("possible junk") and the
   EHB (0x80) and HAM (0x800) bits of the low word are still honoured.
   The CAMG value is ignored for PBM.

   After the BODY is decoded, every pixel index of an indexed image is
   checked against the number of colors in the CMAP. An index that has
   no color is reported with its pixel position.


   3.2. Decoding FORM PBM

   For 8-bit images the IFF row is the width rounded up to the next
   multiple of 2. For 24-bit images the IFF row is w * 3 bytes, also
   rounded up to an even number of bytes. The padding bytes are read
   but never transferred to the image.

   Uncompressed BODY: its size must be exactly the row size times the
   height; any difference is an error.

   ByteRun1 BODY: the decoder reads packets until each row is
   complete. A control byte 0..127 introduces a literal run of
   (control + 1) bytes; 129..255 introduces a repeat of the next byte
   (257 - control) times; 128 is a no-operation. A packet is checked
   against the declared BODY size and against the remaining width of
   the current row, so a packet that crosses the end of a row, a
   truncated stream or a row that does not complete are reported as
   errors rather than read out of bounds. If one single byte remains
   after the last row it is treated as the pad byte of section 2.6.
   Any other leftover data is an error.

   An 8-bit PBM must have a CMAP. A 24-bit PBM does not need one,
   except when it declares color-key transparency (see 3.6).


   3.3. Decoding FORM ILBM

   Planar geometry. Each plane of a scanline is word-aligned
   (ceil(width / 16) * 2 bytes). The BODY holds, for each scanline,
   the color planes 0..n-1 followed by the mask plane when masking is
   1. With ByteRun1 each plane row is decoded separately and must
   produce exactly one plane row of data; a NOP packet (128) is
   skipped. For an uncompressed BODY the size must match the planar
   size exactly (with the masking-1 exception of section 2.6).

   Indexed images (1 to 8 planes). The planes are combined into one
   index per pixel (plane 0 is the least significant bit) and the
   indices are validated against the CMAP (with a SHAM or CTBL chunk,
   also against the 16 registers that the table defines, section 3.4).

   CMAP expansion. Old writers store a 4-bit palette in the high
   nibble of each byte. For ILBM with at most 6 planes, if every
   component has its low nibble clear and at least one is non-zero,
   the palette is expanded by nibble replication (0xA0 becomes 0xAA)
   and a warning is printed. The expansion is skipped when BMHD
   Flags bit 7 (BMHDF_CMAPOK) is set. It is never applied to PBM, to
   7- and 8-plane ILBM (AGA, 8-bit palettes) or to 24-plane ILBM.

   Extra Half-Brite (EHB). Indicated by CAMG bit 0x80. IFF2PNG uses
   at most the first 32 CMAP entries, builds entries 32..63 as the
   same colors with each component shifted right by one, and accepts
   indices in the second half only where the corresponding base color
   exists. EHB with more than 6 planes is rejected. A CMAP with more
   than 32 entries produces a warning.

   HAM. Indicated by CAMG bit 0x800; only 6 planes (HAM6) and 8 planes
   (HAM8) are accepted. Each pixel value is split into a control field
   and a data field: 4 data bits in HAM6, 6 in HAM8. Control 00 takes
   the color from the palette; 01 modifies blue, 10 modifies red and 11
   modifies green, keeping the other two components of the previous
   pixel. At the start of every scanline the held color is palette
   entry 0. A modified component is the data value expanded to 8 bits
   by bit replication (HAM6: value * 17; HAM8: (value << 2) | (value >>
   4)). A palette reference outside the real colors of the CMAP is an
   error. If the CMAP has fewer entries than the nominal base (16 for
   HAM6, 64 for HAM8, 32 for HAM8 + EHB), a warning is printed. The
   result is a true color image; the palette is discarded. With a SHAM
   or CTBL chunk the base palette changes on every row (section 3.4).
   If both the HAM and EHB flags are set, the EHB-expanded palette is
   used as the HAM base (this is IFF2PNG's own interpretation of the
   combination).

   Files without CAMG. An ILBM with 6 planes, no CAMG chunk and a CMAP
   of 1 to 16 entries is decoded as HAM6, as suggested by the ILBM
   specification, with a warning. An ILBM with 6 planes, no CAMG chunk
   and exactly 32 CMAP entries is decoded as EHB, with a warning. With
   any other CMAP size such a file is a plain indexed image (unless it
   has a SHAM chunk, see below), as is any other depth without CAMG. A
   6-plane ILBM without CAMG that has a SHAM chunk, and that the CMAP
   rules above did not classify (no CMAP, or more than 16 entries that
   are not the 32 of EHB), is decoded as HAM6 with a warning, because
   SHAM means Sliced HAM. These assumptions are heuristics and do not
   depend on -strict.

   Deep ILBM (24 planes). Planes 0..7 are the red bits, 8..15 the
   green bits and 16..23 the blue bits, least significant first. No
   palette is needed; with masking 2 the CMAP is kept only to resolve
   the transparent color.

   Mask plane (masking 1). The mask plane is read as alpha: a set bit
   gives 255, a clear bit gives 0. The image is then written as RGBA
   (PNG color type 6, 8 bits per channel). An indexed or EHB image
   with a mask plane is converted to RGB through its palette first;
   the validity of every index is verified. HAM and deep images with a
   mask plane are also written as RGBA. RGBA images are never
   converted to indexed and have no PLTE or tRNS chunk.

   Color-key transparency (masking 2) is ignored with a warning for HAM
   output (the HAM colors are computed, so the key index has no
   meaning) and for per-line palette output (section 3.4).


   3.4. Per-line palettes (SHAM and CTBL)

   Some Amiga programs change the color registers while the screen is
   being drawn, so that every scanline can have its own palette. Two
   IFF extensions store these palettes in an extra chunk, and IFF2PNG
   decodes both:

   - SHAM (Sliced HAM): a palette for every scanline of a HAM picture,
     normally HAM6.

   - CTBL (NewTek): a palette for every scanline of a Dynamic Hires
     picture (hires, normally 4 bitplanes) or of a Dynamic HAM
     picture.

   Neither chunk belongs to the IFF standard and there is no official
   specification: IFF2PNG follows the layout used by the common Amiga
   picture converters. The chunks are recognized automatically; no
   option is needed.

   Chunk layout. A palette is 16 colors of one big-endian word each,
   0000RRRRGGGGBBBB (4 bits per component, as in the OCS hardware
   palette), that is 32 bytes. IFF2PNG scales every component to 8
   bits by multiplying it by 17 (0x0F becomes 0xFF).

     CTBL   The palettes, one per scanline, without any header. Rows
            are never skipped, not even on an interlaced picture.
     SHAM   A version word (0) followed by the palettes. On an
            interlaced picture (CAMG LACE flag, 0x4) there is one
            palette for every two scanlines: an even row y loads
            palette y/2 and an odd row keeps the palette of the row
            above. Otherwise there is one palette per row.

   Application. Before each row is decoded, the palette stored for that
   row replaces color registers 0..15 (all of them, entry 0 included).
   Registers 16 and above keep their CMAP value. With EHB the
   half-bright registers 32..47 are derived again from the new
   registers 0..15, while registers 16..31 and their half-bright copies
   48..63 keep the values of the CMAP. A row that has no palette in the
   table (the table is shorter than the picture) keeps the palette of
   the previous row. The CMAP only supplies the registers that the
   table does not define, so it is optional when the pixels use
   registers 0..15.

   - Indexed pictures (1 to 8 planes, including Dynamic Hires): every
     pixel index is replaced by the color it has in its own row. An
     index is valid if it is below the number of CMAP entries or
     below 16, whichever is greater (with EHB, the usual EHB rule for
     the second half of the registers applies). An invalid index
     stops the conversion with "Pixel at N references color index X,
     but only M palette colors are defined (CMAP + SHAM)".

   - HAM pictures (HAM6 and HAM8): the HAM decode of section 3.3 uses
     the palette of the row for its base colors, and the held color at
     the start of each row is entry 0 of that palette. As in a plain
     HAM picture, a code-0 pixel whose index has no color is an error.
     HAM6 has a base of 16 colors; HAM8 has 64, of which the table
     redefines the first 16. That CTBL defines only 16 registers also
     for HAM8 is an assumption: no authoritative description of Dynamic
     HAM8 was found, so a HAM8 picture needs a CMAP of 64 entries for
     the other registers.

   Mode selection. The picture is treated as HAM, EHB or indexed
   according to its CAMG chunk, exactly as without per-line palettes,
   and a SHAM chunk does not change that: if the CAMG declares neither
   HAM nor EHB, the picture is decoded as an indexed picture with
   per-line palettes, and a warning says so. Only when there is no
   CAMG at all is a 6-plane picture with a SHAM chunk assumed to be
   HAM6 (section 3.3).

   Output. The picture is decoded to 24-bit RGB (32-bit RGBA when it
   has a mask plane, masking 1) and then follows the normal pipeline:
   with at most 256 distinct colors it is reduced to an indexed PNG
   (section 3.5), otherwise it is written as a truecolor PNG. Sliced
   HAM and Dynamic Hires pictures usually have far more than 256
   colors, so they normally come out as truecolor. Color-key
   transparency (masking 2) is ignored with a warning, because the
   transparent index has no fixed color; a mask plane is honored.

   Anomalies. A damaged or unexpected SHAM or CTBL chunk is never an
   error and -strict does not change that: the problem is reported
   with a warning and the picture is decoded as well as possible.

     - A CTBL chunk shorter than 32 bytes, or a SHAM chunk shorter
       than 34, is ignored: the picture is decoded with its CMAP.
     - Trailing bytes that do not make a whole palette are ignored.
     - A SHAM version other than 0 is decoded as version 0.
     - If both chunks are present, CTBL is used.
     - A duplicate chunk is ignored (the first one is used), and so
       is a chunk that follows BODY.
     - A table with fewer palettes than rows: the remaining rows keep
       the last palette (the warning gives the number of rows covered).
     - In FORM PBM and in 24-plane ILBM the chunks are ignored,
       because there is no palette to change.

   Not interpreted. The NewTek DYCP chunk is listed by the -v log but
   its content is not used. The PCHG chunk (palette changes defined by
   line) is not supported: it is skipped like any other unknown chunk,
   and the picture is decoded with its CMAP only.

   With -v the log reports, in section [4], which chunk was applied,
   how many palettes it holds, how many image rows they cover and
   whether the interlace rule was used, and every warning issued about
   the chunks, also when the chunk was applied; if a SHAM or CTBL chunk
   was present but not applied, the reason is given.



   3.5. Palette handling

   All images that end up with 256 or fewer distinct colors are
   written as indexed PNG files. The steps are the following.

   Reduction from true color. A 24-bit image (PBM 24-bit, deep ILBM,
   the RGB result of a HAM decode, or the RGB result of a per-line
   palette decode) is scanned with a hash table of its colors. If the
   image contains no more than 256 distinct colors, a palette is built
   and the pixels are replaced by indices; otherwise the scan stops at
   the 257th color and the image stays true color. The palette produced
   this way is unique by construction.

   Palette compaction. The palette is rewritten so that it contains
   only the colors used by the image. For example, five colors in use
   give a 15-byte PLTE chunk. Pixel indices are remapped to
   0..(colors - 1). With more than 16 colors the colors keep their
   original relative order (ascending original index).

   Ordering of small palettes. For indexed images with at most 16
   colors in use IFF2PNG chooses the order of the palette in order to
   minimize the index differences between horizontally adjacent
   pixels. More similar adjacent indices produce lower-value filtered
   bytes, improving DEFLATE compression efficiency.

   - With 1 or 2 colors, the more frequent color goes first.

   - With 3 to 16 colors, the algorithm builds an adjacency matrix
     over the colors in use: for each pair of horizontally adjacent
     pixels (never across the end of a row) the counter of the pair is
     incremented. Finding the best order is equivalent to a
     travelling-salesman problem on the adjacency graph, which
     IFF2PNG solves heuristically:

       . one starting color for each color in use, in decreasing order
         of frequency;
       . for each starting color, a greedy path that at each step goes
         to the unvisited color with the highest value of
         (adjacencies with the current color) - 5 * (difference
         between the original indices of the two colors), so that
         colors close in the original index are favored;
       . 2-opt optimization: for each pair of edges in the path, it is
         evaluated whether reversing the intermediate segment
         improves the total score; if so, the reversal is applied;
       . among all the paths, the one with the highest total score
         gives the new order.

   The reordering does not modify the visual content of the image:
   pixel indices are remapped consistently with the new palette order,
   and the transparent color index, if any, is remapped along with it.

   De-duplication of identical colors takes place before compaction
   and ordering (see section 2.4).


   3.6. PNG color depth selection and transparency

   IFF2PNG automatically selects the output PNG format based on the
   number of unique colors present in the image:

     Up to   2 colors  ->  1 bit per pixel, indexed
     Up to   4 colors  ->  2 bits per pixel, indexed
     Up to  16 colors  ->  4 bits per pixel, indexed
     Up to 256 colors  ->  8 bits per pixel, indexed
     More than 256     ->  24 bits per pixel (true color, no palette)
     Mask plane        ->  32 bits per pixel (RGBA, no palette)

   The number of colors is counted after the optional merge of
   duplicate entries. For depths below 8 bits, IFF2PNG performs pixel
   packing: multiple palette indices are compacted into each byte,
   with the leftmost pixel in the most significant bits, in
   conformance with the PNG specification. Each scanline starts on a
   byte boundary. The PNG color type is 3 for indexed images, 2 for
   true color and 6 for RGBA; interlacing is never used.

   Color-key transparency (masking 2). IFF2PNG tracks the declared
   transparent color through every conversion step -- 24-to-8-bit
   palette construction, merging, compaction and ordering -- and emits
   it as a PNG tRNS chunk.

   - Indexed images: the tRNS chunk has one entry for each index up to
     the final index of the transparent color: 255 (opaque) for the
     preceding ones, 0 (fully transparent) for the transparent one.
     If no pixel of the image uses the transparent index, no tRNS
     chunk is emitted.

   - 24-bit images reduced to indexed: the transparent color is looked
     up in the CMAP and then in the new palette. If the CMAP is
     missing or too short, or if the color does not occur in the
     image, a warning is printed, the transparency is dropped and the
     image is converted without tRNS rather than marking the wrong
     color as transparent.

   - True color images that stay true color (more than 256 colors): the
     tRNS chunk carries the RGB value of the CMAP entry that the
     BMHD points to. A valid CMAP entry is required; without it the
     conversion stops with an error.

   - HAM output and per-line palette output: transparency is ignored
     (warning).

   - Mask plane (masking 1): see section 3.3; the alpha channel carries
     the transparency.


   3.7. PNG filter selection

   IFF2PNG separates filter selection into two levels: a fast per-row
   heuristic, and a whole-image competition between complete
   filtering strategies, decided by actual compressed size rather
   than by heuristic estimate alone.

   At the per-row level, IFF2PNG evaluates the five filters defined
   by the PNG specification:

     Filter 0 (None)     No transformation.
     Filter 1 (Sub)      Difference with the pixel to the left.
     Filter 2 (Up)       Difference with the pixel in the row above.
     Filter 3 (Average)  Difference with the average of the pixel to
                         the left and the one above.
     Filter 4 (Paeth)    Paeth predictor based on three neighboring
                         pixels.

   The distance to the "pixel to the left" is 1 byte for indexed
   images (including the packed 1, 2 and 4 bit depths), 3 bytes for
   RGB and 4 bytes for RGBA.

   For each filter the sum of the absolute values of the resulting
   bytes (read as signed 8-bit values) is calculated, used as an
   estimate of the compression cost. Three heuristic corrections are
   applied, as integer percentages. They compound when more than one
   applies:

   - Filter None receives a penalty weight (88/100), to compensate for
     its tendency to be falsely favored by the absolute sum metric.
   - The filter selected in the previous row receives a bonus
     (95/100), to favor consistency between consecutive rows and
     reduce the Huffman coding cost of filter types. For the first
     row the "previous" filter is None.
   - Filter Up receives a bonus (90/100) when a previous row exists,
     the PNG depth is 8 (indexed 8 bit, RGB or RGBA) and the sum of
     the absolute byte differences from the previous row is below
     four times the row size, a condition frequent in images
     generated by X-VESA. For indexed images with fewer than 8 bits
     per pixel the bonus is never applied.

   The filter with the lowest score after corrections is selected for
   that row (the lower filter number on equal scores); this is the
   "Adaptive" candidate.

   At the whole-image level, IFF2PNG does not commit to the Adaptive
   candidate by default. It also builds the entire image filtered
   uniformly with a single fixed filter:

     Candidate 0   Adaptive (filter chosen per scanline)
     Candidate 1   All-None
     Candidate 2   All-Up
     Candidate 3   All-Sub     (only if PNG depth is above 2 bits)
     Candidate 4   All-Paeth   (only if PNG depth is above 2 bits)

   With a 1 or 2 bit PNG depth only candidates 0, 1 and 2 exist; in
   every other case, including true color and RGBA, all five are
   tested. Candidates whose filtered bytes are identical to an
   earlier candidate are detected and compressed only once: the
   others receive a copy of its result. Every distinct candidate is
   fully DEFLATE-compressed, and the candidate that yields the
   smallest actual compressed output, not the smallest heuristic
   score, is the one written to the PNG file (on equal sizes the
   lower candidate number wins). With -v the log reports the
   winning strategy and the number of scanlines using each filter.


   3.8. DEFLATE compression

   IFF2PNG implements a complete DEFLATE encoder without dependencies
   on zlib or any other external compression library, offering two
   interchangeable parsing engines selected by the -cx option (see
   section 2.2). Both work on a 32 KiB window with matches of 3 to
   258 bytes, searched with a hash of four bytes and hash chains.

   - the two-pass lazy matcher (-c1..-c4):

       Pass 1 is a greedy parse that uses a flat cost estimate (9 bits
       per literal, 10 bits plus the distance extra bits per match)
       only to count symbol frequencies; code lengths are built from
       them.

       Pass 2 parses the data again using those code lengths to
       estimate the bit saving of each match. After finding a match at
       the current position, it looks at the match at the next
       position (one-step lazy evaluation); if the next match offers a
       larger estimated saving, it emits a literal and advances one
       position before emitting the best match instead. A match that
       already reaches 258 bytes and could be extended is emitted
       without the lazy comparison, so that long runs become
       consecutive matches of 258 bytes.

       The search depth is the maximum number of hash-chain entries
       examined, set by -c1..-c4. After a match of 128 bytes or more,
       16 extra steps are charged against the limit; with -c1 and -c2
       the search also ends once a match of 32 bytes or more has been
       found and more than a quarter of the limit has been used.
       Positions inside a match longer than 16 bytes are indexed with a
       stride of 3 (-c1) or 2 (-c2) instead of every position, to save
       time.

   - the Ultra parser (-cx):

       A greedy pass gives the initial symbol frequencies. Then two
       dynamic-programming passes follow. In each of them, for every
       reachable position, the minimum cost of reaching it is
       computed, with the code lengths of the previous pass as the
       cost model (a symbol without a code is charged 11 bits for
       literal/length and 7 bits for distance); at each position the
       literal, and the matches of every length up to the longest
       found, are relaxed. At the end the cheapest path is followed
       backwards to produce the tokens, and the code lengths are
       rebuilt from that path for the next pass.

       The cost minimized is the sum of code lengths and extra bits.
       Block headers, the run-length coding of the code lengths and
       block splitting are not part of it. For each position, only
       the lengths strictly longer than the best length already seen
       while walking the chain are relaxed: a later candidate with a
       shorter distance code but a length not greater than the best
       is not considered, a deliberate heuristic since nearer
       distances usually have no worse distance codes. The chain is
       limited only by the search depth (no extra steps after long
       matches, no early stop). In the DP passes every position is
       indexed.

       The matches found in the first DP pass depend only on the data,
       not on the cost model, so they are recorded and replayed in the
       second pass. If the record would exceed the cache limit (128
       MiB on 64-bit builds, 16 MiB on 32-bit builds) the chains are
       walked again; the output is the same either way.

   Both engines feed the same downstream stage. Huffman table
   construction uses the Package-Merge algorithm to build canonical,
   length-limited prefix codes (maximum 15 bits for the literal/length
   and distance alphabets, maximum 7 bits for the run-length-encoded
   code-length alphabet, as required by RFC 1951). Every table built
   is verified against the Kraft-McMillan equality before being used;
   an internal inconsistency is treated as a fatal error rather than
   emitted as a possibly undecodable stream.

   Each block is written as the smaller of the dynamic Huffman
   representation (BTYPE=10, with its header) and the fixed Huffman
   representation (BTYPE=01), which wins on short or very regular
   blocks.

   If, after a full encoding pass, the resulting stream would be
   larger than an equivalent stored (BTYPE=00, RFC 1951 section 3.2.4)
   representation of the same data, IFF2PNG discards the compressed
   stream and falls back to stored blocks of up to 65,535 bytes
   instead, guaranteeing the compressor never expands its input by
   more than the small, fixed stored-block framing overhead.

   The produced stream is a complete zlib stream (CMF/FLG header +
   DEFLATE data + Adler-32 checksum), directly usable as the content
   of the PNG IDAT chunk. The whole image is written in a single IDAT
   chunk.


   3.9. Multi-block DEFLATE and adaptive splitting

   IFF2PNG can encode the IDAT stream as more than one DEFLATE block,
   instead of a single block. This targets images where different
   regions have genuinely different statistical character -- a busy
   title bar next to a flat status panel, for instance -- and where a
   single shared Huffman table is a compromise that fits neither
   region well.

   The token stream produced by the DEFLATE parser is searched
   recursively for beneficial split points, with these limits:

   - at most 8 blocks in the final stream, and a recursion depth of at
     most 3;
   - a block is never shorter than 48 tokens, and a span shorter than
     96 tokens is not split at all;
   - within a span, IFF2PNG probes candidate split positions by
     combining three sources: the points where the difference
     between the literal/length histograms of two adjacent windows of
     tokens is large and not decreasing (to catch abrupt local
     shifts; at most 24 such points), an evenly spaced grid of 16
     positions, and a hill-climbing refinement of the best candidate
     found, trying offsets of up to 32 tokens on either side;
   - for each candidate, the bit cost of encoding the two resulting
     blocks separately (each with its own Huffman header) is computed
     and compared with the cost of the single, unsplit span; a split
     is accepted only if it saves more than 16 bits, and the two
     halves are then themselves considered for further splitting,
     within the limits above.

   To keep the search fast, the token histograms are stored as
   cumulative checkpoints, so that the cost of a range of tokens needs
   only two table rows plus a small number of tokens, and the number
   of candidate positions examined has little effect on the running
   time. The counts are exact.

   Because the accept/reject decision is made on the modelled bit cost
   of the alternatives compared at that step, and not on a rough
   estimate, a single decision cannot make the model's cost worse than
   not splitting at that point. The search is however a heuristic,
   and it does not look for a global optimum: whether the accumulated
   effect of several decisions beats a single large block by a
   meaningful margin, is neutral, or occasionally trails a
   differently tuned strategy on some images, is a property of the
   candidate search and of the specific image. Matches are produced
   before the split, so a match may refer to data that lies in an
   earlier block. -nbs is always available for comparison.

   Block splitting is not performed by the fast pre-pass of -cx
   pruning (section 2.5), and a candidate that is kept from that
   pre-pass is therefore a single-block stream.


   3.10. Multithreaded architecture

   The filter candidates described in section 3.7 are compressed
   independently rather than one after another. On Win32 and Win64,
   IFF2PNG launches one thread per distinct candidate (at most five)
   via _beginthreadex when compiled against a multithreaded C runtime,
   or via CreateThread otherwise; on POSIX systems (Linux, macOS and
   other Unix-like systems) the pthread API is used. On any other
   platform, or when no supported threading API is available at
   compile time, the same candidates are compressed sequentially, one
   after another, on a single thread. The banner shows
   "Multi-Thread" or "Single-Thread" accordingly. If a thread cannot
   be created the conversion stops with an error.

   Each candidate is compressed with its own private working memory
   (hash chains, token buffer, Huffman workspace), so no candidate's
   compression state is shared with another's; only a small number of
   global items -- the memory-allocation calls, the progress
   indicator and the deferred warnings -- are shared, and these are
   protected by a mutex (a Win32 critical section or a POSIX mutex)
   on every platform where threads are actually used. The final
   selection is made only after all candidates have finished, by
   comparing sizes in a fixed order. This design means that a
   multithreaded run and an equivalent single-threaded run produce
   the same PNG output: the number of threads used changes how fast
   the conversion completes, never what it produces.

   With -cx pruning (section 2.5) the candidates are compressed twice
   in parallel: first all with the fast engine, then only the
   surviving ones with the DP parser.


   3.11. Memory usage and limits

   IFF2PNG needs, besides the input file and the decoded image, one
   DEFLATE workspace for each distinct filter candidate compressed at
   the same time (up to five with threads, one in sequential builds).
   Each workspace needs approximately 11 bytes for every byte of
   filtered data (hash chain, token buffer, output buffer), about 23
   bytes with -cx (cost and path tables), plus a fixed part of a few
   hundred kilobytes, the histogram index of block splitting (a few
   megabytes) and, with -cx, the match cache described in section 3.8.

   Each workspace is limited to 2 GiB on 64-bit builds and to 512 MiB
   on 32-bit builds. An image that would exceed the limit is rejected
   with an error message (which suggests trying without -cx when -cx
   was selected). The size arithmetic is checked: an image whose
   buffers do not fit in 32 bits is reported as an error.

   The maximum image size allowed by the IFF header is 65,535 x
   65,535 pixels; the practical limit is the available memory.

   8.1. Differences from PBM2PNG V19.1

   IFF2PNG replaces PBM2PNG. The command line of version 1.0 of the
   family (IN OUT [-c1..-c4]) continues to work unchanged. The main
   differences with PBM2PNG V19.1 are:

   - Input: besides FORM PBM, IFF2PNG reads FORM ILBM (1 to 8 planes,
     EHB, HAM6, HAM8, SHAM, CTBL, 24 planes, mask plane) and the CAMG
     chunk. The program is no longer limited to X-VESA screenshots.

   - Output: a mask plane produces an RGBA PNG; PBM2PNG had no such
     case.

   - Options: the new options are -ndedup, -fdedup, -nprune, -strict
     and -v. The option -bs no longer exists (block splitting is the
     default; -nbs remains) and is rejected as unknown.

   - Parsing: three writer quirks are tolerated by default (section
     2.6), and bytes after the end of the FORM are ignored. PBM2PNG
     treated any trailing byte as a corrupt file; -strict restores
     the strict behavior for the three quirks. A 24-bit PBM row is
     rounded up to an even number of bytes, as an 8-bit row is.

   - DEFLATE: lazy matching is one-step (not three-level); blocks may
     be fixed-Huffman when shorter; block splitting is limited to 8
     blocks, depth 3 and a 16-bit minimum gain, and is documented as a
     heuristic; the -cx parser is a cost-model search with two DP
     passes and a match cache and is not described as exhaustive.

   - Filters: the fixed Sub and Paeth candidates are added when the
     PNG depth is above 2 bits (PBM2PNG added them only in -cx mode).

   - Palette: the PLTE chunk always holds exactly the colors in use
     (ascending original order above 16 colors), and identical
     colors can be merged (-ndedup, -fdedup).

   - Console: the chosen filter and the number of blocks are no longer
     printed on the command line; they are in the -v log.

   - -cx pruning, which has no counterpart in PBM2PNG.


   8.2. Changes in V20.7.x

   V20.7.1:

   - New: a warning when a SHAM chunk is found in a picture whose CAMG
     declares neither HAM nor EHB; the picture is decoded as an
     indexed picture with per-line palettes (section 3.4).

   - Changed: every warning about SHAM and CTBL chunks (duplicate,
     after BODY, trailing bytes, version, both present, short table)
     is also kept in the -v log, in the "Per-line palette notes" line
     of section [4], even when the chunk was applied.

   - Changed: the "Palette table" line of the log now reads "N
     palettes in chunk (covers M of H rows)".

   - The conversion of every picture is unchanged.

   V20.7.0:

   - New: SHAM and CTBL chunks (Sliced HAM, Dynamic Hires, Dynamic
     HAM): the palette changes on every scanline (section 3.4).

   - New: a 6-plane ILBM with a SHAM chunk and no CAMG chunk is
     decoded as HAM6, with a warning (section 3.3).

   - New: the -v log shows the CAMG display flags (HIRES, LACE) in
     section [2] and the per-line palette lines in section [4].

   - Fixed: the -v log now reports the 24-to-8 reduction also for HAM
     pictures that were reduced to an indexed palette.

   - Pictures without SHAM and CTBL chunks are decoded and encoded
     as before.


   8.3. General notes

   IFF2PNG was developed as a support tool for X-VESA. The IFF-PBM
   format chosen by X-VESA for its screenshots is optimal for
   generation in pure assembly language -- simple structure, no library
   dependencies, ByteRun1 compression implementable in a few dozen
   instructions -- but is not directly readable by virtually any
   graphics tool currently in use on PC. IFF2PNG solves this problem by
   producing standard PNG files with optimized compression, and extends
   the same service to the planar IFF-ILBM images of the Amiga,
   including the HAM, EHB, Sliced HAM and Dynamic Hires modes.

   The IFF2PNG DEFLATE encoder was written entirely from scratch in
   C89, without making use of zlib or any other compression library.
   This choice is motivated by the distribution context: IFF2PNG
   must be a self-contained and portable executable, free of external
   dependencies.

   Between version 1.0 and version 20.7.1 the converter grew from a
   single-block, single-threaded DEFLATE implementation for PBM files
   into a multithreaded compressor offering two interchangeable parsing
   strategies, whole-image filter competition decided by real
   compressed size, adaptive multi-block partitioning of the DEFLATE
   stream itself, palette de-duplication decided by measurement and a
   decoder for the planar Amiga formats, including the per-line
   palettes of Sliced HAM and Dynamic Hires -- while the command-line
   contract of version 1.0 continues to work unchanged, with every new
   feature reachable through additional, optional switches.

   For the typical images produced by X-VESA -- screens with reduced
   palettes, text graphics and bars -- IFF2PNG produces PNG files
   significantly smaller than generic converters, thanks to palette
   compaction and ordering, whole-image filter competition and, when
   requested, the -cx parsing engine.
